5 of Swords

Five of Swords

In shortYou can win this, and it will cost more than it is worth.

Upright

hollow victorywinning at a costconflictself-interestdefeat

This is the argument won by fighting dirty, the point proved at the expense of a relationship, the victory that leaves you standing alone in a field. Sometimes the card puts you on the losing side instead. Either way the advice is the same: consider letting this one go.

Reversed

making amendswalking awayreleasing a grudgeopen conflict

The conflict resolves or you escape it. Amends made, a grudge dropped, a fight walked away from before it became unwinnable. Less happily, it can mean hostility that was covert is about to come out into the open.

In the pictureA smirking figure gathers up the swords while two others walk away, heads down. The sky is ragged and torn. He has all the weapons and no company — the image is very clear about which is worth more.

The turn

Concede the point. Being right is currently the expensive option.
